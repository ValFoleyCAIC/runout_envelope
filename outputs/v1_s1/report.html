Methodology

Each scenario runs through AvaFrame com1DFA (Voellmy dense-flow DFA model) with per-scenario friction (μ, ξ) and release density (ρ) from its params.json. Entrainment is handled as a constant-depth layer (0.3 m) applied over an auto-detected α–β corridor downslope of the release. Forest is represented as a resistance area. Reach masks (flow depth ≥ 0.01 m) are stacked cell-by-cell to produce the probability raster.

The probability value at any cell is the fraction of the 1 scenarios whose reach mask includes that cell. This is a conditional probability: P(reach | this set of plausible release scenarios). It is not an annual or return-period probability and cannot be interpreted as such without external frequency information.

Limitations

  • Small ensemble (n = 1). With 20 scenarios, probability granularity is 5% and CIs on low-probability contours are wide.
  • Entrainment as constant depth (0.3 m). Defensible starting value for dry surface slab (Vera Valero et al. 2016; Bartelt et al. 2018 Vallée de la Sionne calibrations), but not spatially varying. Upgrade path: layered snowpack model output.
  • Friction is a placeholder, not derived from snowpack state. The Voellmy friction parameters (μ, ξ) in params.json are currently identical across all scenarios — they are not yet derived from the upstream snowpack model. The values used (μ=0.155, ξ=1500.0) are on the lower end of typical dry-snow Voellmy ranges (μ ~ 0.15–0.30, ξ ~ 1000–4000), which biases toward longer runouts — a conservative direction for road-impact analysis. Upgrade path: link μ/ξ to per-scenario snowpack state (density, temperature, layer structure).
  • Road buffer = 1.0 m. Road is treated as centerline buffered by this distance. Three-lane centerlines with different road widths warrant per-segment half-widths (future upgrade).
  • α–β fallback. When α outside [15°, 45°], entrainment area reverts to flow-accumulation buffer. Logged per scenario in scenarios_summary.csv.
